Coding and modifier reference by service Expand details

Psychiatry and outpatient visits

Common CPT: 90791, 90792, 99202–99205, 99211–99215, 99495, 99496.

Typical modifiers/POS: 25 when separately identifiable and supported; 95 or payer-required telehealth modifier; POS 02 or 10 based on encounter setting and payer policy.

Psychotherapy

Common CPT: 90832, 90834, 90837, 90839, 90840, 90846, 90847, 90853.

Typical modifiers: 95 for telehealth when required; 59/X modifiers only when documentation and payer edits support distinct services.

IOP, PHP, residential and detox

Common HCPCS: H0010, H0015, H0017, H0018, H0019, H0035, H2036, S0201, S9480.

Claim focus: authorization, per diem or units, bill type/revenue configuration, attending/rendering provider, admission/discharge data, and medical necessity.

Behavioral therapy, SUD and case management

Common HCPCS: H0004, H0005, H0006, H2020.

Claim focus: individual versus group, plan-of-care, staff credentials, authorized units, taxonomy, diagnosis, and payer-specific coverage.

ABA / autism therapy

Common CPT: 97151–97158, 0362T, 0373T.

Typical modifiers: HO, HN, HM, HP, U1–U9, GT/95 only where payer contracts require them. Rendering provider, supervision, units, place of service, and authorization must align.

TMS, Spravato and urine drug screening

Common CPT/HCPCS: 90867–90869, G2082–G2083 or payer-specific Spravato billing, G0480–G0483, 80305–80307.

Claim focus: prior authorization, treatment-session documentation, drug/service billing method, CLIA and laboratory rules, units, and payer policy.

Code and modifier use varies by payer, contract, state, setting, documentation, and current guidance. This portfolio describes experience and is not a universal coding instruction.

Sanitized case studies

Problem → Action → Result

01

High-denial behavioral health group practice

Problem: Denials above 17% driven by recurring eligibility, authorization, coding, and submission errors.

Action: Classified denial roots, corrected front-end checks, introduced claim scrubbing and QA, revised SOPs, and tracked the top denial categories weekly.

Result: Denials reduced to 6% or less, first-pass claims exceeded 98%, and days in AR moved from 45 to 32.

02

Telehealth modifier and place-of-service errors

Problem: Virtual psychiatry and psychotherapy claims were rejected or denied because modifier and POS combinations did not match payer rules.

Action: Mapped payer-specific telehealth requirements, corrected affected claims, trained billers, and added a pre-submission validation step.

Result: Recovered affected claims, reduced repeat errors, and improved clean-claim consistency.

03

Invalid primary diagnosis and aged AR

Problem: Old claims remained unpaid because the primary diagnosis was invalid, unsupported, or sequenced incorrectly.

Action: Compared clinical documentation, payer edits, diagnosis sequencing, and authorization; corrected claims or appealed when documentation supported payment.

Result: Cleared aged balances and prevented the same diagnosis pattern from recurring.

04

Missing claims and unbilled encounters

Problem: Schedule, encounter, and claim reports did not reconcile, leaving completed services unbilled.

Action: Reconciled scheduling, attendance, documentation, charge entry, and clearinghouse acknowledgment; created and submitted missing claims.

Result: Increased practice collections and established a routine missing-claim control.
